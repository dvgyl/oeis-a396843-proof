## Source statements and exact scope

The official entries [A378580](https://oeis.org/A378580) and
[A378581](https://oeis.org/A378581), inspected on 24 October 2026, define two
constant-one generating functions by

\[
 A_-(x/A_-(x))=\Theta(x),\qquad
 A_+(xA_+(x))=\Theta(x),\qquad
 \Theta(x)=1+2\sum_{j\ge1}x^{j^2}.
\]

Both entries conjecture the same coefficient pattern at every positive index:

\[
 [x^n]A_\pm(x)\equiv
 \begin{cases}
  2\pmod4,& n\text{ is a square},\\
  0\pmod4,& n\text{ is not a square}.
 \end{cases}
\]

The formalization proves both statements, including the “if and only if” and
the integer divisibility clause. The stronger entire-series statements are
\(A_-(x)\equiv A_+(x)\equiv\Theta(x)\pmod4\).
The constant terms are one and are deliberately excluded from the square-index
claim. No analytic radius of convergence is asserted; the separate question
about the radius in A378580 is outside these results.

The official entries still presented these assertions as conjectures when
inspected. Bounded web searches for the exact identifiers together with
“proof”, and for the two functional equations together with “theta” or
“mod 4”, did not identify an earlier proof. This is a record of the checks
performed, not a claim of comprehensive priority research or global novelty.

The related [A196523 entry](https://oeis.org/A196523) was also checked. Its
comment dated 13 October 2026 credits Amol Bhave with a proof for the family
\(F=x+xF^{\langle\ell\rangle}\), giving coefficients one modulo \(\ell-1\).
The displayed argument is specific to that iterated family and its geometric
comparison series. It does not directly state the constant-one theta
substitution results proved here, and the inspected page contains no linked
general theta theorem. This additional comparison narrows one relevant
prior-art question without establishing exhaustive novelty.

## Construction over the integers

The Lean objects are actual Mathlib formal power series. The definition does
not insert the desired coefficient residues, and none of the final theorems
assumes an unconstructed solution of the source equation.

We first prove a general fact over **every commutative ring**. If \(T(0)=1\),
there is exactly one normalized series
\(G=x+g_2x^2+g_3x^3+\cdots\) satisfying

\[
 G(G(x))=G(x)T(x).
\]

To see the triangular structure, suppose two normalized series first differ
in degree \(r\), with difference \(d x^r\) to that order. Changing the outer
series in the self-composition contributes \(d x^r\), because the inner
series has linear coefficient one. Changing the inner series contributes
another \(d x^r\), because the outer series also has linear coefficient one.
Multiplication by the fixed unit \(T\) changes the right side by
\(d x^r\). Thus the residual \(G\circ G-GT\) changes in degree \(r\) by
exactly \(d\), not by a scalar that would need division. Every next
coefficient is therefore determined integrally, and existence follows by
well-founded triangular recursion. The same comparison proves uniqueness.

The implementation proves the leading-coefficient facts separately before
invoking the project's general triangular-recursion theorem. Degrees zero
and one are checked directly.

Write this uniquely constructed series as \(G=xU\). Then \(U(0)=1\), and

\[
 G(G)=G\,U(G)=GT.
\]

Cancellation gives \(U(xU)=T\). Cancellation is justified over arbitrary
coefficient rings: multiplication by \(x\) is injective, and \(U\) has a
genuine multiplicative inverse because its constant term is one. No field
assumption or cancellation of arbitrary nonzero elements is used.

Call this solution \(\operatorname{Plus}(T)\). Conversely, every
constant-one solution of \(A(xA)=T\) gives the normalized solution \(xA\)
of the preceding equation. Thus \(\operatorname{Plus}(T)\) exists and is
unique, in particular over the integers.

For the quotient equation, set

\[
 U=\operatorname{Plus}(T^{-1}),\qquad
 \operatorname{Minus}(T)=U^{-1}.
\]

Here all inverse symbols denote **multiplicative unit inverses**, rather than
compositional inverses. From \(U(xU)=T^{-1}\), substitution respects
multiplication and therefore

\[
 U^{-1}(xU)=T.
\]

Since \(x/\operatorname{Minus}(T)=xU\), this is precisely the required
quotient equation. The same operation takes any solution back to
\(\operatorname{Plus}(T^{-1})\), proving uniqueness.

Lean implements the multiplicative inverse using
`PowerSeries.invOfUnit A 1`, proves the two inverse identities, and proves
that inversion commutes with valid substitution and coefficient maps. The
theorem `a378580_quotient_identity` explicitly gives
\(A_-(x)\,(x/A_-(x))=x\), so the quotient notation has no implicit
interpretation left to the reader.

The canonical integer sequences are now
\(A_- = \operatorname{Minus}(\Theta)\) and
\(A_+ = \operatorname{Plus}(\Theta)\). Each has its original functional
equation and a full existence-and-uniqueness theorem over \(\mathbb Z[[x]]\).

## The modulo-four calculation

Work in \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)[[x]]\) and write
\(\Theta=1+2S\), with \(S=\sum_{j\ge1}x^{j^2}\). The crucial calculation
is valid for any series \(S\), independent of square support.

Let \(B=x(1+2S)\). Since \(2B=2x\), multiplication by two gives
\(2B^k=2x^k\) for every nonnegative integer \(k\). This follows by
induction, or by factoring the difference of powers. Applying it term by
term to a formal substitution yields

\[
 2S(B)=2S(x).
\]

Every coefficient in this formal substitution is a finite sum because
\(B(0)=0\). Consequently

\[
 \Theta(x\Theta(x))=1+2S(x\Theta(x))=1+2S(x)=\Theta(x).
\]

Thus \(\Theta\) itself solves the plus equation over \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\).
The general uniqueness theorem shows
\(\operatorname{Plus}(\Theta)=\Theta\) in this ring. Furthermore

\[
 \Theta^2=(1+2S)^2=1,
\]

so \(\Theta^{-1}=\Theta\). The construction of the minus solution gives

\[
 \operatorname{Minus}(\Theta)
 =\operatorname{Plus}(\Theta^{-1})^{-1}
 =\Theta.
\]

Reduction from the canonical integer solutions commutes with all the
defining operations. Lean proves this by mapping the original source
equations and invoking the same uniqueness theorems in the target ring.
The calculations over \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) therefore determine the
residues of the actual integer sequences. Finally, the explicit coefficient
definition of \(S\) identifies the positive square positions.

## Independent finite corroboration

`scripts/verify_theta_substitution.py` directly implements each source's
coefficient recursion from its original functional equation. It separately
implements the normalized recursion \(G\circ G=GT\) used in the formal
construction and checks the two constructions agree.

The default run checks all coefficients through degree 64 using exact
integers. It verifies both literal source equations at those degrees, checks
the quotient's multiplicative inverse, matches all 24 displayed A378580
terms and all 22 displayed A378581 terms, and checks both residue clauses
through the same bound. Results are in
`validation/theta_substitution_numeric.json`.

These finite computations check the translation between the source and the
formal definitions; they are separate from the Lean proofs for every index.
